## What the user asked
Two new static pages matching the redesigned site: `/guide` — seven practical
techniques for asking Jev good questions, each with a short heading, a couple of
plain sentences, a small picture or before/after, and a source link, closing on a
three-column "code / LLM / Jev" picture; and `/cost` — a client-side
decision-cost calculator built on pure arithmetic in a lib with its own tests.

## What changed
**`/guide` — "Ask Jev well".** Seven numbered techniques, written in our own
words: rebuild the option list every step; send independent questions together
(fetch first, then ask); put the requirement in the question text and describe
every option; give evidence rather than summaries; confidence is not accuracy;
narrow long candidate lists in stages; and a confident answer never proves an
action happened. Four are illustrated with small SVG figures (stale vs rebuilt
option lists, chained vs batched questions, a calibration plot, a filter →
Score → Choice funnel) and three with before/after code panels. Each carries its
source links — the Browser Use jev-ultrafast repo, the TypeSafe docs pages named
in the brief, and the matching pattern records in this atlas. The page ends with
the three-column "stays in code / stays with an LLM / goes to Jev" picture, links
to `/fit` and `/map`, and a flat source list.

**`/cost` — decision cost.** Four editable inputs (decisions per day, input
tokens per decision, price per million input tokens, your current cost per
decision) produce cost per day, per month, and per 10,000 decisions, plus a
two-bar SVG comparison against the current route and a one-line verdict. The
price defaults to the single exported constant `VENDOR_INPUT_PRICE_PER_M = 0.042`,
labelled with the site's `Vendor Claim` chip, linked to the pricing claim record
(resolved from the knowledge layer, not hardcoded) and to the OpenRouter listing,
with a reset button once it is edited. The fixed caveat — track cost per
completed task, because a cheap wrong decision can cost more than the call —
sits under the chart. Everything is arithmetic in the browser; no network.

Both pages are in the sitemap, in the nav "More" menu ("Ask well", "Cost"), and
in the mind map as leaves (`what:guide` under "What Jev is", `fit:cost` under
"Will it fit my workflow?"). `test/mindmap.test.ts` gained an assertion that
those two leaves exist under the right parents, since nothing else would catch a
leaf silently dropping out.

No inline `style=` anywhere: layout uses the site's Tailwind conventions, and the
figures use CSS modules plus SVG presentation attributes. Verified against the
production CSP (`style-src 'self'`) rather than dev.
